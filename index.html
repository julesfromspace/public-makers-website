Public Makers

Our manifesto

I. The Deadlock

Germany is reaching the limits of a model it still believes can continue. The promise we grew up with was simple. Study, work, play by the rules, and a stable life follows. That promise built our institutions and set our expectations. It has not disappeared, but for the generation now entering adulthood, it no longer holds true.

What remains is the habit of doing the job by the book. People still follow the rule even when they can see it no longer fits, even when following it makes things worse, because whoever breaks from it is the one who answers when something goes wrong. So they shrug, say you know how it is, and comply. Responsibility for the common good gets passed along, up to the state or sideways to another office, until no one feels it is theirs. On its own, each person's caution makes sense. Together, all that caution is what holds everything still. Nothing moves, because moving has a price and waiting has none.

There is a comforting story about how this ends without anyone having to act. The older generation retires, AI takes up the slack, a few posts are cut, and the state slims its way back to health. It will not happen that way. The state is where a society makes the decisions that bind all of us, and those decisions rest on judgment. A machine can produce, and it can know more than any of us. What it cannot do is judge: tell which problem actually matters, what makes sense, what is worth doing. That is what the state needs most, and the people who can do it and have somewhere else to go are exactly the ones it is losing.

II. What It Takes

It is not that the capable people are missing. It is that the two roles on offer pull them in half. One thinks like a civil servant, the other like a founder, and each carries half of what it takes. The civil servant has continuity, memory, the long view, and a feel for how durable change actually happens. The founder has speed, vision, and the nerve to start before everything is clear. Inside the institutions, the one who thinks like a civil servant is pushed to play it safe until the drive they came in with is gone, and the one who thinks like a founder finds no place where their way of working counts. The fault is never the people. The two halves are simply kept apart.

What it takes is for them to meet in one person. A civil servant who keeps the founder's readiness to start. A founder who keeps the civil servant's patience for what only pays off slowly. You do not have to change jobs. You have to change how you do the one you have: to pair the drive to start with the patience to see it through, and to point both with judgment, the sense of what is actually worth building. That sense is earned, not installed, by doing the work and seeing what it does for the people it is for. Few manage it, in a single person across a single career, and that rare thing is what the next decade runs on. That is the gap we step into.

III. Who We Are

The people who close that gap, we call Public Makers.
Public Maker is not a job title, it is an identity. You can be a civil servant and a Public Maker, a founder and a Public Maker, a teacher or a parent and a Public Maker. Whatever the role, the identity runs underneath it. The common good is not the property of one office; it is the thing we had stopped believing was ours to make. The same outlook can sit in someone who has spent twenty years inside an institution and in someone who only just began, and it is the same thing in both.

It is more than any one of us can carry alone. One person who moves can be ignored, managed, or worn down. Enough of us who move together, and find each other, become something the deadlock cannot absorb, and a stalled culture turns from below, until the ones above have no choice but to expect it too.

What the culture takes from people is the sense that anything they do will matter. The identity gives it back. You stop waiting to be asked, stop measuring yourself by the room you are in, and start counting by what you make move. That is not a temperament you are born with. It is what happens to people once they stop carrying it alone.

If you recognise yourself in any of this, the identity was already yours. We are not waiting for the culture to change. Find the others. Start.
